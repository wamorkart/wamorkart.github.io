Tanvi Wamorkar

I am Tanvi, a postdoctoral researcher at Stanford University and a member of the ATLAS and H1 Collaborations, where I use machine learning to study the smallest building blocks of nature.

My research has three threads: how to get more out of collider data, by correcting it for detector effects without throwing information away; how to make large machine learning models small and fast enough to run on the hardware that decides which collisions the LHC keeps; and how foundation models work for physics — what inductive biases a network should have built in, and what it learns on its own when given enough data.

Before Stanford, I was a postdoc at UC Berkeley and Argonne National Lab, and I did my PhD at Northeastern University on the CMS experiment. Along the way I’ve worked with a lot of amazing scientists and students, and that’s one of my favourite parts of the job.

I enjoy challenging, interdisciplinary problems and building tools that make scientific work faster and better. I’m also always picking up new software tools to speed up my own analyses.

I’m always happy to talk about potential projects, ideas, and analyses. Feel free to shoot me an email if you want to get in touch!
